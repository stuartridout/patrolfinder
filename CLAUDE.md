# patrolfinder

Quiz SPA sorting people into the four 1907 Brownsea patrols (Wolves, Bulls, Curlews, Ravens). The UK's activity at the 26th World Scout Jamboree, Poland 2027, for an international audience; first used at Gilwell Reunion 2026 and tested next with adults at an all-adult weekend, but the Jamboree is the target. Lives at wsjpatrol.com. The look is Direction C "Signal" from the October 2026 Claude Design handoff.

The app is one file: `index.html`, no build step, the lockup, fleur and fonts embedded as data URIs, all screens JS-rendered into `#app`. A PWA needs real URLs, so `manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `icons/` and `CNAME` ship alongside it, `viewer.html` is the live wall display for the screen on the stand, and `backend/` holds the Azure Function App. The Publish workflow ships exactly that set and nothing else. `fonts-src/` holds the font files the pages inline. `posters/` holds the four A4 teaser posters (PDF) and the script that renders them from `index.html`'s own fonts and patrol marks - decode-verifying every QR - so it stays private to main and cannot drift from the app. The posters are still the Reunion design.

Rules that matter:
- Patrol colours and 1907 rosters are historical and verified. Do not change them. Keep "probably" on Simon Rodney, in every language, and keep the honesty line's meaning: real patrols, real names, real colours, the personalities just for fun. The colours are the Patrol Shoulder Knots the boys wore, as the Heritage Team checked them in October 2026: Bulls red, Curlews green, Ravens black, Wolves yellow and black.
- Wolves and Ravens are both black, so Wolves is black with a yellow border and yellow accents and Ravens is pure black with none. Wolves' yellow goes on what marks the patrol - the mark, the "You're a Wolf" plate, the main button, the patrol name - and running text stays cream. The border is an 8px frame round the result screen, a frame round every Wolves card, a 16px frame round the TV panel, and a ring on every swatch and bar, because at that size it is the only thing telling Wolves from Ravens. `--wolves-accent` in CSS, `PATROL_ACCENT` in JS, and only Wolves has one.
- Wolves yellow is never type, or a border, on cream or white: it all but disappears. Each `.p-<patrol>` class sets the same CSS names (`--p`, `--ink`, `--mark`, `--plate`, `--btn` and so on) and screens read those, never a patrol by name; `--ink` is the patrol as type on cream.
- `--bulls` is a patrol colour, not "green". Success ticks and the privacy note use `--green`.
- The knot is drawn as it was worn: Wolves split yellow and black down the notch, one tail each.
- The patrol marks are the site's own silhouettes, traced to vectors and checked to within half a pixel of the artwork: `HEADS` in both pages, an SVG `<symbol>` on screen and a `Path2D` on the cards. Stuart chose these over the design's simplified marks; do not swap them. Outline versions (reveal, empty states) are the same path with a stroke.
- There are two sets of the same seven questions, 1907 and today, chosen on the intro. There is one `PATTERN` array and both sets read it - same patrol behind each option, same weights, same order - so a Wolf in 1907 is a Wolf today by construction. That equivalence is the argument the site makes. The words live in `STR`, per language and per set.
- The two sets look different by ground and ghost year: cream paper with "1907", sky blue with "2027". Everything else is one template.
- Question wording belongs to the people who own it. Q6 (water) is the Safety Team's wording, verbatim, and it is one `water` entry per language, shared by both sets, because two copies is how one of them eventually gets edited. Its translations need the Safety Team.
- Answer buttons are navy and white only. No patrol colour, animal or knot ever goes near an answer: the order is shuffled per person and the answers are recorded to check the quiz for bias.
- Five languages: English, French, Polish, Spanish, Arabic. All five are switched on (Stuart, 25 Oct 2026), and a phone opens in its own language when it is one of them. English is the source; the other four were drafted in session and no native speaker has read them yet, and their water question has not been through the Safety Team - so a correction from either is a string edit, made as soon as it arrives. A language appears in the menu only when its `ready` flag in `LANGS` is true (and in `FIND` in `viewer.html`); set false, it is reachable by `?lang=xx` alone, with a draft banner. Every string falls back to English. With one ready language the language button is left out, and the top bar's `1fr auto 1fr` grid keeps the title centred anyway. Whether patrol names are translated is undecided; the drafts translate them.
- Arabic is right to left: `dir="rtl"` on the root, flex does most of the mirroring, the pennant notch and the Back arrow flip, the patrol marks mirror, and the ghosted years stay left to right. Arabic is set in Cairo throughout and about two sizes up. Numbers, dates and plurals go through `Intl` with the language's locale; Arabic uses Latin digits (`ar-u-nu-latn`).
- Every run records its question set and its language. Without them the per-question bias figures average different questions together. Runs with no set are shown as "question set not recorded" rather than assumed.
- Official logo rules: Poland 2027 lockup on a clean light background (the white masthead bar; a white plate on the TV), never rotated, recoloured or crowded. Scouts fleur 38px or more; the reversed single-colour fleur on the cards is a permitted use.
- The intro has one job: pick a question set. It carries the heading, one line, the two set buttons and nothing else to press - no patrol tiles. Four coloured squares above the choice read as four buttons (Stuart, testing the live site, October 2026), so the patrols wait for the result. On a phone the heading and choices share one navy block that stretches to the screen so the choices sit low; from 600px the column centres in the navy. The `(min-width:960px) and (max-height:780px)` block must stay after the plain `(min-width:960px)` block.
- The reveal is held back a beat: the seventh answer lands on a navy screen where the four marks flick through for about two seconds and a ring fills, then the result cuts in. The run is recorded there, so the count is of finished quizzes. Never put a patrol colour on that screen - it would give the answer away. Reduced motion skips the flicker.
- Every screen change starts at the top. `toTop()` is called first in every render function.
- No `opacity` on text sitting on a patrol colour. It composites the whole subtree and a child cannot set it back. Curlews green is the tight one: full-strength cream is 4.67:1.
- The result plate re-inks per patrol: Wolves a yellow plate with black type and a black border, the others their own colour with cream type and a cream border. Never one fixed plate colour.
- Audience: young people aged 14-17 from everywhere, their leaders and staff, many reading in a second language, standing up, on a phone. Fewer words, plain and literal: no idioms, puns or wordplay, which do not survive translation.
- The patrol card is drawn in the browser and the photo never leaves the device. Say so plainly on the screen, in words anyone reads once and believes. If that ever stops being true, the copy changes first.
- The patrol wall is opt-in, reportable by anyone, and deleted seven days after `REUNION_ENDS`. All of it is enforced in `backend/src/functions/api.js`, not promised in a policy. `REUNION_ENDS` must match in `index.html` and the Function App's settings, because the app shows people the date it computes: it is 2027-08-24, so 31 August 2027. The name dates from Gilwell Reunion and is the Azure setting's name.
- Cards go up straight away by default. The `moderate` switch turns human checking back on, and the wall panel's wording follows it (`#factUp`): if nobody is checking, the app does not say anyone is.
- Four switches (`wall`, `uploads`, `cards`, `moderate`) live in the team's console at `<api>/console`. The app defaults every one to on and caches the last known answer, so an unreachable API never hides a feature. Anything a switch hides needs `data-needs`, and `[hidden]` needs `!important` here because half the layout sets `display`.
- Every completed quiz records its seven answers, the four scores, any tie-break, the set and the language, so the quiz can be checked for bias. It records nothing about the person. The result screen says what is kept and why, and that sentence changes before the storage does, not after.
- `API_BASE` and `REUNION_ENDS` at the top of the script are the only switches. The Pavilion email sign-up went with the Reunion version. Never fake a number: until the API answers, the tally is a dashed empty bar with "waiting for signal" and a key with no figures.
- The photo is reframed on the picture itself: one finger drags, two pinch, the wheel zooms on a laptop. The canvas is focusable and takes arrows and +/- so the keyboard keeps both.
- `photoRegion()` is the single definition of where the photo sits on each card. The drawing and the pan clamp both need it, and separate numbers drifted apart once.
- Cards: profile (1080 square, everything inside the circle, Wolves' yellow ring inside it), post (4:5, photo in the top 60% with a slanted cut, the mark cropped off the bottom corner), story (9:16, top and bottom 250px clear for Instagram's buttons), wall (1080 square). Over a photo the caption gets a cream plate, because the photo could be any colour. The fleur rides the post card's slanted edge in a patrol disc, so it only appears with a photo. Names and captions are width-capped with `fitFont()` so they stop short of the mark; widening a cap puts text through an animal.
- Every upload to the wall is rendered in the `wall` format whatever is on screen, so the wall is one design. It carries the patrol name, the mark (or the photo) and "Brownsea 1907" and stops: a wall card is looked at about 170px wide.
- The wall panel in the studio is an invitation, not a consent form. All the facts are on it - anyone can see it, when it goes up, that anyone can report a card, when it is deleted, that it is optional - as four calm lines with icons. If it ever reads like a warning, nobody will use it.
- The wall on the phone pulls down to refresh. `body.s-wall` sets `overscroll-behavior-y:contain`, or the browser's own pull-to-refresh reloads the page and lands you back on the intro, because a screen here is a render and not a URL. The pull moves `.pullwrap`, never `.screen`: `.screen` carries a filled animation, and a filled animation beats an inline transform. There is a Refresh button too, because a laptop has no pull.
- A refresh that fails leaves the cards that are already up. At a stand with patchy signal, a blank wall is worse than a slightly old one.
- The Report control is a 44px cream square with a flag, in the card's top start corner.
- Pennant buttons cut their notch with a pseudo-element painted `--ground`, so the focus ring is not clipped. Anywhere the ground changes (the sky set, navy panels) sets `--ground` to match, or a cream notch shows on blue.
- Focus rings: house red on light grounds, cream on patrol colours and navy, yellow on Wolves black.
- The icon is Baden-Powell in his campaign hat, traced from the silhouette Stuart supplied, cropped to the head and shoulder and bled off the bottom edge. It is not original artwork and it is not drawn from scratch - a hand-drawn head in a wide-brimmed hat turns into a plague doctor's mask at icon size. `icons/favicon.svg` carries the path and the two framings the eight PNGs are rendered at; never hand-edit the PNGs.
- `viewer.html` is the wall on the stand's screen: 16:9, full screen, read-only. The cream rail stays put: lockup, "Patrol wall", the total, four patrol rows with bars showing each patrol's share, and a big QR with "Find your patrol", which cycles through the ready languages (`FIND`, kept in step with `LANGS`). The right panel shows one patrol at a time on its own colour for about 20 seconds: up to eight cards in a 4x2 grid, and past eight a two-row strip at the same big cell that holds the first screenful 4s, slides sideways at about a column a second to the last card, holds that, then rotates - the dwell stretches so every card gets its moment. Density was tried twice and big cards with a slide beat more cards every time. Squarer than 13:9 drops to three columns rather than shrinking four. The QR is inlined as SVG and verified by decoding a render, not by eye. A poll that changes nothing must not repaint the panel (`computeSpotKey`), or every refresh would restart the hold and the slide. No connection means the whole panel goes navy with "Waiting" and the rail shows dashes, never zeros; a failed refresh keeps the cards already up; a patrol whose card just landed jumps the queue. Cards get a cream inset edge so a card in the patrol's own colour does not melt into the panel; Wolves cards carry their own yellow frame. It duplicates the fonts, the patrol marks, the lockup and the fleur from `index.html` on purpose - it is a second page, and sharing them would mean a build step. Change them in both places or they drift.
- The viewer's rail is sized in `--ru` - 1vw capped at its 16:9 worth of height, `min(1vw, 1.7778vh)` - never plain vw. In vw, any window wider than 16:9 ran the stack off the bottom and put the tally rows through the QR. At true 16:9 the two units are equal, so the TV picture is untouched.
- The service worker caches a navigation as the app shell only when the path is `/` or `index.html`. `viewer.html` is a navigation too, and before that guard existed it would have been cached as the shell, handing offline phones the wall display instead of the quiz.
- Bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` whenever `index.html` changes, or returning visitors keep the old copy.
- Deploys: push to `main`, the Publish workflow rewrites `gh-pages`, GitHub Pages serves it at wsjpatrol.com. CDN caches about 10 minutes. The console and the API change only when someone runs `backend/deploy.sh`.
- Before shipping visual changes, render real screenshots at 390x844@2x, 360 and 1440 for every state and critique them, in English and at least once in Arabic and Polish, plus all four card shapes x four patrols x with and without a photo, and the TV at 1920x1080 and a laptop window. `bin`-less: the harness lives in the session scratchpad pattern (playwright, a mock API, axe-core, forced states via `renderResult(id)` / `renderCardStudio(id)` / `renderLog()` / `renderReveal()`, drafts via `?lang=`).
